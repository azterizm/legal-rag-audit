ORDER GRANTING DEFENDANT'S MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT AND DISMISSING CASE WITH PREJUDICE

Before the Court is Defendant Megacorp International Inc.'s Motion for Summary Judgment pursuant to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 56. Defendant seeks summary judgment on all claims asserted in Plaintiffs' First Amended Class Action Complaint on the ground that Plaintiffs' claims are barred by the applicable statute of limitations.

I. PROCEDURAL AND FACTUAL BACKGROUND

Plaintiffs Jane Doe and John Roe initiated this putative consumer class action on January 10, 2025, alleging that Megacorp International Inc. engaged in deceptive marketing, false advertising, and breach of implied warranty regarding the battery efficiency and durability of its flagship consumer electronic devices.

According to the allegations in the complaint, Plaintiffs purchased their respective Megacorp devices in August 2020. Plaintiffs allege that by November 2020, they observed substantial battery degradation and performance throttling. In December 2020, public tech journals published detailed investigative technical reports documenting the identical hardware defect across Megacorp's product line.

Defendant Megacorp moved for summary judgment, arguing that Plaintiffs' causes of action accrued no later than December 2020, when the widespread public disclosures put Plaintiffs on inquiry notice of their potential claims. Because Plaintiffs did not file suit until January 2025, Megacorp argues that all claims are barred by California's three-year statute of limitations under Code of Civil Procedure § 338(d).

II. STATUTE OF LIMITATIONS ANALYSIS AND TOLLING ARGUMENTS

Under California law, a cause of action for fraud or statutory consumer deception accrues when the plaintiff discovers, or has reason to discover, the cause of action. See Fox v. Ethicon Endo-Surgery, Inc., 35 Cal. 4th 797, 807 (2005). A plaintiff relying on the discovery rule to toll the statute of limitations must specifically plead facts showing (1) the time and manner of discovery and (2) the inability to have made earlier discovery despite reasonable diligence.

Here, Plaintiffs contend that the statute of limitations was equitably tolled under the fraudulent concealment doctrine. Plaintiffs assert that Megacorp published software patches between 2021 and 2023 that disguised battery degradation metrics, thereby lulling consumers into believing performance issues were temporary software glitches.

The Court finds Plaintiffs' tolling arguments unavailing. The record evidence demonstrates beyond dispute that the December 2020 technical publications provided sufficient facts to put a reasonable consumer on inquiry notice. Diligent investigation at that time would have revealed the basis for Plaintiffs' claims.

The class action lawsuit was dismissed with prejudice. The plaintiffs failed to file within three-year statute of limitations. The motion for summary judgment by the defendant, Megacorp, was granted in full.

III. MOOTNESS OF CLASS CERTIFICATION PREREQUISITES

Because summary judgment is granted in favor of Defendant on all individual threshold claims on statute of limitations grounds, the Court need not address the pending class certification prerequisites under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 23.

Specifically, Plaintiffs' motions regarding Rule 23(a) numerosity, commonality, typicality, and adequacy of representation, as well as Rule 23(b)(3) predominance and superiority, are rendered moot. It is well settled that where named class representatives' individual claims fail as a matter of law prior to class certification, class certification must be denied and the underlying class claims dismissed. See Cowen v. Bank United of Texas FSB, 70 F.3d 937, 941 (7th Cir. 1995); Corbin v. Time Warner West Division, 475 F.3d 1089, 1092 (9th Cir. 2007).

IV. CONCLUSION AND ORDER

For the reasons set forth above, IT IS HEREBY ORDERED that:
1. Defendant Megacorp International Inc.'s Motion for Summary Judgment is GRANTED in full.
2. Plaintiffs' First Amended Complaint is DISMISSED WITH PREJUDICE.
3. The Clerk of Court shall enter final judgment in favor of Defendant and close this file.

IT IS SO ORDERED.
