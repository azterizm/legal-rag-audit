STATUTE BETA: COMPREHENSIVE LABOR RELATIONS AND ESSENTIAL SERVICES ACT

AN ACT to regulate collective bargaining procedures, safeguard essential public services, establish dispute resolution mechanisms, and govern worker rights and employer responsibilities across public and private labor sectors.

ARTICLE 1. SHORT TITLE AND SCOPE
This Statute shall be known and cited as "Statute Beta (Labor Code)". The provisions of this Code shall govern all employer-employee relationships, collective bargaining units, labor organizations, and dispute resolution bodies within the jurisdiction.

ARTICLE 2. LEGISLATIVE INTENT AND POLICY
It is hereby declared to be the public policy of the State to promote peaceful, prompt, and equitable settlement of labor disputes, to protect the right of employees to organize and bargain collectively, and to prevent disruptions to essential services vital to public safety and health.

ARTICLE 3. DEFINITIONS
As used in this Statute:
(a) "Essential Workers" means employees whose continuous services are critical to public safety, health, and welfare, including healthcare professionals, emergency medical responders, firefighters, law enforcement personnel, municipal water treatment operators, and grid utility engineers.
(b) "Labor Dispute" means any controversy concerning terms, tenure, or conditions of employment, or concerning the association or representation of persons in negotiating terms of employment.
(c) "Strike Notice" means formal written notification issued by a certified labor organization indicating intent to initiate work stoppage or job action.
(d) "Board" means the State Public Employment Relations Board established under Article 6.

ARTICLE 4. COLLECTIVE BARGAINING RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS
Employees shall have the full right to self-organization, to form, join, or assist labor organizations, and to bargain collectively through representatives of their own choosing. Employers and labor unions share a mutual duty to bargain in good faith regarding wages, hours, benefits, and workplace safety conditions.

ARTICLE 5. In the event of a labor dispute involving essential workers, mandatory binding arbitration must commence within 14 days of the strike notice.

ARTICLE 6. COMPOSITION OF THE ARBITRATION AND RELATIONS BOARD
The State Public Employment Relations Board shall consist of five members appointed by the Governor with the advice and consent of the Senate. Two members shall represent organized labor, two members shall represent public and private employers, and one neutral public member shall serve as Chair. Members shall serve staggered four-year terms.

ARTICLE 7. ARBITRATION PROCEDURES AND EVIDENTIARY RULES
During binding arbitration under Article 5, the arbitration panel shall hold formal hearings, receive documentary evidence, take sworn witness testimony, and evaluate:
(1) Comparison of wages and working conditions with similar municipal sectors;
(2) Overall financial capability of the employer;
(3) The interest and welfare of the public; and
(4) Changes in consumer price indices and cost-of-living metrics.

ARTICLE 8. INJUNCTIVE RELIEF AGAINST UNLAWFUL WORK STOPPAGES
If a labor organization representing essential workers initiates a strike or work stoppage without providing required statutory notice or refusing to enter mandatory arbitration under Article 5, the Attorney General or affected employer may petition the Superior Court for an immediate temporary restraining order and permanent injunction.

ARTICLE 9. APPEALS AND JUDICIAL REVIEW
An arbitration award rendered pursuant to Article 5 shall be final and binding upon all parties. Judicial review of an arbitration award is strictly limited to grounds of fraud, corruption, arbitrator misconduct, or excess of statutory authority, and must be filed in the Appellate Court within 30 days of award issuance.

ARTICLE 10. SEVERABILITY AND EFFECTIVE DATE
If any provision of this Code or its application to any person or circumstance is held invalid, the remainder of the Statute or the application of such provision to other persons or circumstances shall not be affected thereby. This Code shall take effect 90 days after passage.
