SUPREME COURT OF THE UNITED STATES

No. 22-9014

ROBERT JONES,
Petitioner,

v.

STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE,
Respondent.

On Writ of Certiorari to the Supreme Court of New Hampshire

Decided October 24, 2023

JUSTICE ALITO delivered the opinion of the Court.

This case requires us to decide whether administrative regulatory filing deadlines established under state environmental compliance codes are subject to judicial equitable tolling when a regulated entity experiences extraordinary administrative delays.

I. PROCEDURAL HISTORY AND FACTUAL BACKGROUND

Petitioner Robert Jones operates a small commercial fuel storage facility governed by state environmental monitoring statutes. Under Section 404-B of the State Environmental Protection Act, commercial storage operators are required to submit an annual underground storage tank safety disclosure form to the State Department of Environmental Services no later than 5:00 p.m. on March 31 of each calendar year.

In March 2022, Petitioner attempted to submit his annual compliance documentation via the state's online web portal. Due to a server outage occurring on the afternoon of March 31, Petitioner was unable to upload the completed form until 12:04 a.m. on April 1, 2022—precisely four minutes after the statutory cutoff.

The Department of Environmental Services subsequently issued a civil administrative penalty assessment against Petitioner. The agency determined that a late filing of the administrative form incurs a strict liability fine of $500. Petitioner appealed the administrative penalty to the State Environmental Appeals Board, arguing that the electronic server outage constituted an extraordinary event warranting equitable tolling of the midnight deadline. The Board affirmed the fine, concluding that the relevant statutory text contained no statutory waiver or equitable exception.

Petitioner sought judicial review in the State Superior Court, which affirmed the agency order. The State Supreme Court likewise affirmed. We granted certiorari to resolve a split among state high courts regarding the availability of judge-made equitable tolling for strict liability administrative deadlines.

II. STATUTORY INTERPRETATION AND LEGAL ANALYSIS

Our analysis begins, as it must, with the text of the statute. Section 404-B(c) provides: "Any entity failing to submit the required annual compliance disclosure on or before March 31 shall be assessed an automatic administrative fine of $500. No administrative extension shall be granted except as provided in subsection (d)." Subsection (d), in turn, enumerates specific force majeure exceptions for declared natural disasters, none of which encompass website technical glitches.

Where Congress or a state legislature enacts an explicit strict liability scheme with explicit statutory exceptions, courts are not free to engraft unwritten equitable exceptions onto the statutory text. See United States v. Brockamp, 519 U.S. 347, 352 (1997); BP America Production Co. v. Burton, 549 U.S. 84, 95 (2006).

This established a firm precedent that statutory deadlines cannot be bypassed by equitable tolling in this specific jurisdiction. Mandatory administrative deadlines promote regulatory finality, predictable enforcement, and efficient administration across thousands of permit holders.

III. DISSENTING OPINIONS

JUSTICE SOTOMAYOR, with whom JUSTICE KAGAN joins, dissenting.

The Court today elevates mechanical literalism over basic fairness. When a state entity creates an exclusive electronic portal for statutory compliance and that portal collapses due to the state's own server failures, the state should not be permitted to profit from its own technical dysfunction by penalizing citizens who diligently attempted timely compliance.

Under longstanding principles of equity, when extraordinary circumstances beyond a party's control prevent compliance with a filing deadline, equitable tolling operates to prevent manifest injustice. See Holland v. Florida, 560 U.S. 631, 649 (2010). The majority's rigid rule strips courts of their traditional equitable discretion and imposes strict financial penalties on citizens who committed no fault. I respectfully dissent.

IV. CONCLUSION

The judgment of the Supreme Court of New Hampshire is AFFIRMED.
